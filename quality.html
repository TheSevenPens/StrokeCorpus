Why a single clock is not enough

The pen's own timestamp looks like a clock and is not one. On the hardware here it advances a flat 4.166 ms per delivered packet whatever the elapsed time, runs at 0.673 of real time, and resynchronises to real time at every contact transition. It implies a report rate of 240 a second; the real rate, measured against the host's own clock, is 161.6.

So a recording carrying only that timestamp cannot date a reading in real time, and speed computed from it is speed per counter-second. Recordings at format version 5 and above carry a second timestamp taken from the host, and that one is a clock.

It also explains the hover tag. The recorder used to decide how old an airborne reading was using the pen's counter, which runs slow — so readings were discarded that should have been kept, and the approach to a landing came out short. Only format version 6 ages the approach on the host clock.
